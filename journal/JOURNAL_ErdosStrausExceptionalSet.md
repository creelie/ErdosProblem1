Title: An explicit exponent for the exceptional set of the Erdős–Straus equation
Authors: Deep Bhattacharjee (corresponding; itsdeep@live.com, d.bhattacharjee@erl-forschung.de; ORCID 0000-0003-0466-750X) and Ushashi Bhattacharya (rai.bhattacharya.in@gmail.com; ORCID 0009-0002-2254-3914)
MSC2020: 11D68 (primary); 11N36, 11N35, 11N13
Keywords: Erdős–Straus conjecture, Egyptian fractions, large sieve, primes in arithmetic progressions

Cover letter points:
- Vaughan (1970) bounded the exceptional set of the Erdős–Straus equation by N exp(−c (log N)^{2/3}) with no value of c. For the primes in the exceptional set, Dahan's recent sieve (arXiv:2608.24035) gives c = 0.065, and c = 0.0943 with the constant of its sieve sum found by Liu. The paper proves the bound for the whole exceptional set with every c < c₁, where c₁ is given by an explicit integral, is proved to exceed 0.6931 by computations in which every rounding is directed against the bound (0.66571 in closed form; 0.7015 by a Monte Carlo simulation); this is more than seven times the best previous explicit value.
- The residue classes reachable by the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem, from three corners of the triangle of factorisations of (p + 1)/4, give exactly c₀ = 9/(2^{8/3} π^{2/3}) = 0.6607… (Theorem B shows the large sieve can do no better with them). The step past c₀ uses classes of the centre of the triangle, counted with Maynard's two theorems on primes in progressions to moduli beyond √p; the second reaches moduli up to p^{3/5} when the modulus splits into three factors of suitable sizes, and the paper finds such splittings for a positive proportion of the pairs up to the depth 1/10, beyond which none exists.
- The main new steps are the distinctness of the classes from different regions of the factorisations, with explicit descriptions of the coincidences, a divisor rule that places the moduli of the centre in Maynard's range, with the density of its failures computed as an integral, and a certified lower bound for the density of the moduli that split in the deep strips.
- The paper does not claim the Erdős–Straus conjecture.
